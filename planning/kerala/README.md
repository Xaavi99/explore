# Kerala Tailored Experiences — Planning Workspace

This is a **planning document**, not yet wired into the deck generator or the
catalogue. It's the working layer between the raw destination research in
`South_India_Catalogue.md` (attractions, what's included, highlights per stop)
and a client-ready itinerary: real pacing, real stays, real transfer times,
built the way a concierge would actually route a trip.

Scope for this pass: **Kerala only**. The same model — arrival logistics +
pacing rules + a named-stays day-by-day — should extend to Goa, Karnataka,
Tamil Nadu and the island/cross-border journeys once this is validated.

Everything here is a planning draft. Hotel names, drive times and flight
routings reflect real, well-established options as of early 2026, but
**schedules, rates and property affiliations change** — reconfirm live before
quoting a client.

## Arrival logistics: the gateway decision

Every model assumes the client is flying from **Europe or the UAE/Gulf**.
Kerala has three international airports; which one to route through — and
whether to fly a domestic connection or never let a client sit in a car for a
transfer that a 55-minute flight would replace — is the first decision in any
itinerary, not an afterthought.

### Kochi (COK) — the default gateway

Cochin International is Kerala's own hub, centrally placed for almost every
itinerary (Munnar, Thekkady, Alleppey and Kumarakom are all within a half-day
drive), and it's the right default for most Kerala-only trips.

- **From the UAE/Gulf**: genuine nonstop service — Emirates and flydubai from
  Dubai, Etihad and Air Arabia from Abu Dhabi/Sharjah, Qatar Airways from
  Doha, Oman Air from Muscat, Gulf Air from Bahrain. This is the easiest,
  shortest path into Kerala for a Gulf-based client — often under 4 hours
  in the air.
- **From Europe**: there's no reliable, year-round nonstop European service
  into Kochi. The practical fastest path is **one connection through a Gulf
  hub** (Dubai, Doha or Abu Dhabi) — total journey usually 10–13 hours
  door-to-door including the connection, which beats routing through Mumbai
  or Delhi on total time and on jet lag (Gulf connections keep the client
  roughly on a single overnight rhythm).

### Bangalore (BLR) — the better-connected alternative, used carefully

Bangalore has stronger direct long-haul links from several European cities
(Lufthansa from Frankfurt, British Airways and Air India from London, Air
India's expanding continental network, seasonal Finnair from Helsinki). If a
client's home city connects better to Bangalore than to a Gulf hub, it can be
the faster door-to-door option even though Bangalore itself is in Karnataka,
not Kerala.

**The rule**: if we route through Bangalore, the Bangalore→Kerala leg is
**always a domestic flight** (BLR→COK is under an hour, multiple daily
departures on IndiGo/Air India/Akasa), **never a road transfer**. A
Bangalore–Kochi road transfer is 7–8 hours — exactly the kind of unrealistic
travel day this planning model exists to rule out. Bangalore only earns its
place in the routing if it's paired with a genuine Karnataka extension
(Coorg/Mysore) or if it measurably shortens the client's total travel time
from their home city.

### Trivandrum (TRV) — the right call for south-weighted itineraries

Thiruvananthapuram International has its own Gulf connections (Dubai, Abu
Dhabi, Doha, Sharjah) at a similar level to Kochi, plus occasional winter
charter service from the UK/Europe. It's the better gateway when an
itinerary is weighted toward Kovalam, Varkala or a Kerala+Sri Lanka
combination — landing in the south avoids backtracking past Kochi twice.

### Kannur (CNN) & Calicut (CCJ) — the right call for North Kerala only

Kochi is the wrong gateway for a Wayanad/Kannur/Bekal itinerary — it's a
5–6 hour drive from Wayanad. North Kerala has its own two Gulf-connected
airports: **Kannur International**, close to Kannur and Bekal, and
**Calicut International**, roughly 1.5–2 hours from Wayanad. [Model 5](05-north-kerala-explorer.md)
routes arrive-Kannur / depart-Calicut and never touches Kochi at all — the
simplest logistics of any model in this set, and worth using whenever a
Kerala itinerary is North-weighted rather than routed as an add-on to a
Kochi-based trip.

### Departure

Wherever practical, the itinerary should **end near the airport it started
from** or at worst a short (under 2 hour) transfer from it — arriving guests
should never face a long final transfer on a travel day that already includes
a long-haul flight home. All five models below are routed with this in mind.

## Comfort & pacing rules (the "pro concierge" constraints)

These are the rules every model in this folder is built against. They are
non-negotiable defaults; deviate only with a specific reason noted in the
itinerary.

1. **No road transfer longer than ~4 hours.** Kerala's hill roads are
   winding and slow by distance alone; a 130 km drive to Munnar is a genuine
   4-hour undertaking, not a "quick hop." If two stops are further apart than
   that, either add an interim stop, split the drive, or reconsider the route.
2. **A soft-landing day after long-haul arrival.** No sightseeing scheduled
   before midday on arrival day; check in, rest, and start properly the
   morning after. This matters more for European arrivals (2.5–4.5 hour time
   difference from CET, landing after an overnight flight) than for Gulf
   arrivals, but both get the buffer.
3. **Linear routing, no backtracking.** The car should never retrace a road
   it has already driven that trip. Every model below routes as a loop or a
   line from arrival airport to departure airport.
4. **At least one genuinely unstructured day per 5–6 touring days.** Not a
   "light day" with a half-day tour bolted on — a day with nothing booked,
   so the pace doesn't compound.
5. **Altitude and lowland stops are never adjacent without a buffer.**
   Munnar (~1,500 m, cool) sitting directly next to a coastal beach stop
   works better with Thekkady or Kumarakom between them, both climatically
   and logistically, than a single long descent-and-return.
6. **End on the coast.** Every model closes with a beach or backwater stop
   near the departure airport — the lowest-effort, most restorative way to
   end a trip before a long flight home.
7. **Houseboats are one night, not the whole backwater experience.** An
   overnight on a *kettuvallam* is a highlight, not a base — guests move on
   the next morning rather than staying moored for multiple nights, which
   the boats aren't designed for and which flattens the pacing.

## Stays: how properties are chosen

Where possible each stop lists 2–3 real, well-established options across
different tiers (a "reference-quality" pick and one or two alternates), with
a one-line reason. Kerala's serious hospitality names recur across these
models because they're genuinely the right calibre for a private, tailored
trip:

- **CGH Earth** — Kerala's own heritage/eco-luxury group, and the most
  consistent name in this document: Brunton Boatyard (Kochi), Spice Village
  (Thekkady), Coconut Lagoon (Kumarakom), Kalari Kovilakom and Kalari
  Rasayana (Ayurveda-dedicated, Palakkad/Kollam), Marari Beach Resort,
  Tranquil (Wayanad plantation stay). Locally owned, genuinely embedded in
  place rather than an imported resort format — the default recommendation
  wherever it fits the itinerary.
- **Taj/Vivanta/IHCL** and **Kumarakom Lake Resort** — the dependable
  higher-tier alternative in the same towns, for clients who want a familiar
  international standard.
- A short list of independent boutique properties named per-stop below
  (e.g. Windermere Estate in Munnar, Vivenda dos Palhaços in Varkala,
  Neeleshwar Hermitage in North Kerala) where they're genuinely the most
  distinctive option in that location.

**Verify before quoting**: confirm current rates, room categories and that
the property is still operating as described — hospitality portfolios do
change, and this document is a planning reference, not a live inventory.
